# Supplier Payment Terms

Every vendor at Maple Street Roasters has a negotiated payment term recorded in the master vendor sheet. We do not pay on receipt — we pay on terms, and the discipline of that is what keeps our cash position healthy. The bookkeeper runs AP every Tuesday and Friday, and any invoice older than its term gets flagged red on the dashboard.

Vendor terms at a glance:
- Coffee Bean Importers (green coffee): Net-30 from invoice, 2% discount if paid in 10 days
- Local Dairy Partner (milk, cream, yogurt): Net-15, delivered Tue/Fri/Sat
- Packaging Vendors (cups, lids, bags): Net-45, minimum order $750
- Pastry suppliers: COD or Net-7 depending on partner
- Equipment technician: Net-30 on invoices over $500, COD below

We aggressively take the early-pay discount on green coffee since it's our largest line item — 2% on a $9,000 invoice is real money over a year. Coffee Bean Importers knows our volume and has extended the discount window twice; that relationship is one of our few true leverage points. Local Dairy Partner has tighter terms because their margin is thin and they pass fuel surcharges through, so we never let those invoices age. Packaging Vendors get the longest terms because their volume is predictable and they've never pushed back.

All of this feeds the Cash Flow Forecast — see [[Cash-Flow-Forecast]] for how vendor due dates map onto the 13-week projection. When a supplier offers extended terms as a goodwill gesture, we log it but do not budget for it, so a single late payment never quietly resets our baseline.
